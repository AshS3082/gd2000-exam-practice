## 3. Chess and Texas Hold'em

Both are two-player competitive games. Describe how hidden information and
randomness change what the player is actually doing in each. Then give one
concrete design consequence: something you would have to change if you moved
chess's rules to poker's information model, or the other way around.

Chess lacks hidden information as the board lays out all the pieces out for both opponents to see. There is also no randomness as each piece abides by their own rules. This makes the game focus on strategy and prediction. Success is determined by decision making and skill. On the other hand, Texas hold'em involves hidden information and randomness as players cannot see their opponent's cards which leads them to make decisions based on limited information. The randomness factor comes from the cards being dealt, meaning players cannot control which cards they receive. In this game players use probability, bluffing, and observation to predict what their opponent might have in their hand. Unlike chess, even a good decision does not always guarantee a win because luck plays a huge role in Texas Hold'em. 

If chess were changed to use poker's information model, players would no longer be able to see all of their opponent's pieces or moves. The game would need a way to hide certain pieces or information while still making it playable. This would make chess less about calculating every possible move and more about predicting opponents and taking risks. On the other hand, if you switched Texas hold'em to have Chess' information, it would get rid of the hidden cards and the randomness in the game. This would mean that bluffing is eliminated form the game and the game would focus more on calculating the best move and what possible move their opponents would make.

## 5. The game loop

Pick one of: Go, Blackjack, Settlers of Catan, Pandemic, Slay the Spire,
Hades, Minecraft, Stardew Valley, Overcooked, Pac-Man.

Describe its game loop: what the player does, what the game updates in
response, and what feedback the player gets. Then remove one step of the loop
and describe what breaks.

Game: Stardew Valley

The core game loop of Stardew Valley has the player start each day by choosing an activity like watering crops, mining, fishing, talking to villagers, or caring for animals. As the player completes these actions, the game updates by growing crops, using energy and time, earning money and items, increasing friendship levels, and unlocking new areas or upgrades. The player gets feedback through harvested crops, gold, experience points, relationship hearts, new tools, and visible progress on the farm. 

If one of these steps were to be removed, then the game would not progress in the same way. This would lead to players losing the sense of achievement which drives the game.
